RAID Visual Explainer

Learn how RAID or Redundant Array of Independent Disks works through animated storage diagrams. This page compares the most common RAID levels and shows how data is striped, mirrored, and protected with parity.

Quick Comparison

RAID Level Main Technique Minimum Drives Can Survive Usable Capacity Typical Use
RAID 0 Striping 2 0 failed drives All drives combined Scratch disks, temporary high-speed workloads
RAID 1 Mirroring 2 1 drive in a two-drive mirror About 50% Important files, small servers, boot drives
RAID 5 Striping + parity 3 1 failed drive Total minus one drive File servers, shared storage
RAID 6 Striping + double parity 4 2 failed drives Total minus two drives Large arrays, safer archival storage
RAID 10 Mirroring + striping 4 Depends on which drives fail About 50% Databases, virtual machines, high-performance systems
RAID 2, RAID 3, and RAID 4 exist historically but are uncommon in modern everyday deployments. RAID 50 and RAID 60 are enterprise combinations built on RAID 5 and RAID 6 groups.
